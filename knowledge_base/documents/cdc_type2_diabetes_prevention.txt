## Preventing Type 2 Diabetes Overview

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), type 2 diabetes can often be prevented or delayed—particularly if you have prediabetes (blood sugar levels higher than normal, but not yet high enough for a type 2 diagnosis). 

## 1. Make Proven Lifestyle Changes
- Lose a small amount of weight: If you are overweight, losing a modest amount of weight (such as 5% to 7% of your body weight) can significantly lower your risk of developing type 2 diabetes.
- Eat healthy: Focus on making healthy food choices, watching portion sizes, and balancing your nutrition. Choose foods lower in saturated fats, trans fats, added sugars, and sodium.
- Get physically active: Increase your physical activity—aiming for regular movement like brisk walking (at least 150 minutes per week) to help your body use insulin more effectively and lower blood glucose levels.

## 2. Know Your Risk Factors and Get Tested
You can have prediabetes for years without experiencing any clear symptoms. The CDC recommends talking to your doctor about getting a simple blood sugar test if you have risk factors such as:
- Being overweight or having a high Body Mass Index (BMI).
- Being 45 years of age or older.
- Having a parent, brother, or sister with type 2 diabetes.
- Being physically active less than three times a week.
- Having a history of gestational diabetes (diabetes during pregnancy) or giving birth to a baby weighing more than 9 pounds.
- Belonging to a higher-risk racial or ethnic group (African American, Hispanic/Latino American, American Indian, Alaska Native, Pacific Islander, or certain Asian American groups).

## 3. The National Diabetes Prevention Program (National DPP)
The CDC coordinates the National Diabetes Prevention Program, a lifestyle change program specifically designed to help people with prediabetes prevent or delay type 2 diabetes.
- What it offers: A year-long, structured program led by a trained lifestyle coach where participants work in a group setting to learn sustainable habits regarding nutrition, physical activity, stress management, and coping skills.
- The clinical impact: Scientific research demonstrates that participation in structured lifestyle change programs can cut the risk of developing type 2 diabetes by up to 58% (and 71% for individuals over 60 years of age).

## Diagnostic Tests Supported by CDC Guidelines
- Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) Test: Measures your blood glucose after an overnight fast. Normal is under 100 mg/dL; Prediabetes is 100 to 125 mg/dL; Diabetes is 126 mg/dL or higher.
- A1C Test (HbA1c): Measures your average blood glucose over the past 2 to 3 months. Normal is below 5.7%; Prediabetes is 5.7% to 6.4%; Diabetes is 6.5% or higher.
- Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT): Measures blood glucose before and 2 hours after drinking a sugary drink. Normal is under 140 mg/dL; Prediabetes is 140 to 199 mg/dL; Diabetes is 200 mg/dL or higher.
